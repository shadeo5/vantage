Architecture · Engineering

Gear → Subject Matching

How a photographer's kit — their cameras and lenses — becomes a signal the app can act on: telling them which shoots their gear can actually nail, powering the "why this pick — perfect for your fast 35" line, and, later, shaping the nudge.

Doc ADR — gear layer Status Built & wired · annotate live Updated Jul 12, 2026
01

Why gear is load-bearing

Today the app assumes everyone shoots a 35mm. But a kit shapes what you can shoot well. A 600mm telephoto is built for sports and wildlife at a distance; a fast 35mm prime is a street shooter's lens; a macro is for tiny detail. Point a wide-only shooter at a wildlife-telephoto shot and the nudge feels wrong.

This is a north-star lever, not just polish. The app's job is to give a personal reason to go shoot tonight — and "this fits your bag" is one of the least generic things it can say. It also lowers the data bar: gear + light can carry a nudge even where activity data is thin.

Scope

Gear personalization is for experience quality & engagement, not a differentiation claim — a rival (Fotocast) already ships gear-by-type scoring. Our edge is fusing gear with the street / activity dimension, not owning "gear matching" itself.

02

The matching engine

Built and tested in vantage/lib/gear.ts — a catalog of 19 cameras + 24 lenses plus the matcher. The whole idea in one line:

// every lens has a reach (focal length) and a light / subject-isolation
// ability (aperture) — map those to genres, then union across the bag:
genresForKit(cameras, lenses) → Genre[]   // e.g. ["Street","Sports","Details"]

Two real-world wrinkles the engine already handles:

  • Crop factor. The same 35mm lens "sees" differently on a small sensor vs. a large one. The engine converts every focal length to a full-frame equivalent before judging it — so a 33mm on an APS-C body correctly reads as ~50mm.
  • Aperture gating. A "fast" lens (f/2.8 or brighter) unlocks low-light and subject-isolation genres — Street, Portraits — that a slow lens does not.

The genre rules, keyed on the full-frame-equivalent focal length:

Equivalent focalGenresFeel
≤ 20mmLandscape · Architecture · NatureUltra-wide vistas
20–28mmStreet · Architecture · LandscapeWide reportage
28–40mmStreet · ArchitectureClassic street (35mm)
40–60mmStreet (+ Portraits if fast)Normal (50mm)
60–105mmPortraits (if fast)Head-and-shoulders (85mm)
105–135mmSports (+ Portraits if fast)Short reach
135–300mmSports · Wildlife · NatureAction / distance
> 300mmWildlife · NatureSuper-tele reach
Macro lensDetails (always)Plus its focal rules

"Fast" = f/2.8 or brighter. genresForKit() runs these over every lens (and every fixed-lens body), unions the results, and dedupes — the answer is the set of genres your bag covers.

03

The decisions

Three forks sit inside this feature — which is why it earns an ADR. The feature is now wired (annotate-only): A was settled up front, C is resolved for its first step (we shipped annotate), and B remains open.

DecisionStatusReasoning / alternatives
A · How a match is computed Chosen Deterministic focal + aperture rules. Alternatives: a hand-tuned lookup table, or an LLM judging the kit. Rules win for now — transparent, unit-testable, free, good-enough. Revisit if they get unwieldy or a match feels wrong too often.
B · Where gear data comes from Open Curated catalog (built — ~40 items, doesn't scale) vs. an open camera-database API vs. user free-text (flexible but messy to parse). Leaning: curated for launch, free-text + normalization later.
C · How matching meets the feed Annotate shipped The load-bearing one. Filter (hide spots your kit can't shoot — punishing), re-rank (nudge fitting spots up), or annotate only ("fits your 35 / grab a tele"). Shipped: annotate-only — Bag, Today, Lock & Plan name the fitting lens and never hard-filter. Re-rank stays open until there's a real ranker to hook into; overlaps the events / ranking scorer.
Cross-reference

Decision C touches the same scorer described in the Events & Opportunities ADR. Gear should influence ranking, not become a separate parallel system — the "opportunity brain" stays one ranker.

04

Known simplifications

Honest shortcuts in the current engine, documented so they're not mistaken for intent:

  • Phones filed as "1-inch." The sensor-format list has no "phone" class, so flagship phones borrow the nearest one. The equivalent-focal math still resolves correctly to ~24mm via a chosen crop factor — only the sensor label is approximate.
  • Any lens fits any body. genresForKit evaluates every lens on every camera in the kit, ignoring lens mounts — so a phone + a 600mm would over-claim "Wildlife." Over-generous, not wrong for a coverage union; a future pass matches lenses to compatible bodies.
05

Open questions

  • Does gear re-rank the feed or only annotate it? (Decision C — needs the ranking model settled first.)
  • Free-text gear entry: how do we parse "I shoot a Fuji with the nifty fifty" into catalog entries?
  • Should the app ever suggest a genre the user's gear can't do (aspirational "grab a tele"), or only what they own?
  • When the user's declared favorite (Bag style chips) disagrees with what their gear implies, which wins?
  • Match confidence: how sure before we surface "fits your kit," so we're never confidently wrong?
06

Consequences — what wiring touched (shipped)

  • ✅ BagScreen — catalog-backed camera picker (all 19 bodies) + lens chips + a live "Your kit shoots" row.
  • ✅ Persisted gear profile (G1) — camera + lenses saved on-device (AsyncStorage), survives reload.
  • ✅ The "why this pick" line + per-spot fit labels (Today / Lock / Plan) now consume genresForKit / bestLensForGenre — honest, never naming a lens that doesn't fit.
  • ◻ The ranking scorer (Decision C re-rank) and the nudge (E3 · N3) — still ahead; gear-aware copy will feed those when the backend/ranker exist.
