The three players

Same three colors as the diagram. Each card: a plain analogy, what it does, why we can't skip it, and what we actually set up.

🧠
Supabase
The cloud brain & memory
✓ Live
"The app's memory, plus its night shift — the part that keeps thinking about you after you've locked your phone."
Does
Stores your gear + your phone's push address, and (soon) runs the nudge decision on a timer in the cloud.
Why we need it
The app only thinks when it's open. Something has to remember you and decide "is tonight worth it?" while the app is closed. That lives in the cloud — Supabase.
What we did
Built the database (your profile + nudge history), locked it down so only you can see your data, and wired the app to auto-save your gear to it. Already working.
✉️
Expo / EAS
The builder & the messenger
⏳ Building
"The factory that packages the real app — and the post-office clerk who knows exactly which carrier can reach your phone."
Does
Builds the actual installable app, and offers one send-a-notification service that reaches both iPhone and Android.
Why we need it
The browser/preview version can't receive push — you need a real build. And instead of wiring Apple and Google separately, Expo is a single "send to any phone" doorway.
What we did
Logged into your account, linked the project, added the notification code, and kicked off a cloud build for your Pixel. Compiling right now.
📡
Firebase (FCM)
Google's delivery pipe for Android
✓ Done
"The one mail carrier that's allowed through the gate to Android phones. Everyone else has to hand their letters to it."
Does
The actual last-mile delivery of a notification onto an Android device.
Why we need it
On Android, every push — no exceptions — goes through Google's system (FCM). Expo hands the message to Firebase; Firebase drops it on your Pixel.
What we did
Created a free Firebase project, gave the app its ID badge (google-services.json), and gave Expo permission to send through it (a service-account key).

Why so many keys and files?

This is the part that feels confusing. It's really just two ideas: some keys let a thing receive, others let a thing send — and "send/master" keys must stay secret.

The key / fileIn plain termsLives where
google-services.jsonThe app's ID badge for Firebase — lets the app receive Android push.Inside the app (safe to keep in the project)
Firebase service-account keyExpo's permission slip to send through Firebase — lets the server send.Uploaded to Expo only · secret
Supabase anon keyThe app's public library card for the database.Inside the app (public by design)
Supabase service_role keyThe master key to the whole database.Cloud night-shift job only · secret
The rule of thumb

"Receive" keys ride inside the app. "Send" and "master" keys stay locked in the cloud — you never paste those into the app or share them.
