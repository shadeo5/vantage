Light Quality is Genre-Dependent

The nudge scores light on a single quality ladder — golden best, flat worst — inherited from a landscape mockup. But light isn't good or bad; it's a set of creative modes, and how much it should gate a shoot depends on the genre. This ADR replaces the global weight with a per-genre light-sensitivity model, grounded in the research memo.

01

The problem — an inherited landscape assumption

The nudge brain scores light with one global weight (lib/nudge.ts):

BASE_QUALITY = { golden: 1.0, blue: 0.85, flat: 0.55 }
light = BASE_QUALITY[spot.windowType] * timing * weather

That says golden light is intrinsically ~2× more valuable than flat/midday — a landscape value judgment, carried over from the original landscape design comp. The Product Brief already flagged it as a "borrowed signal": "Golden hour is a landscape obsession… half my best street frames are flat noon light with a great subject. Don't down-rank my afternoon."

The incoherence it creates today

The recent weather + shoot-brief work went the other way — cloudFactor doesn't penalize flat light, and the brief actively celebrates "overcast → even-light street." So the app now demotes flat-light street spots in the ranking (0.55 vs 1.0) while celebrating them in the brief. It simultaneously down-ranks and praises the same thing.

02

What the research found

A deep-research pass (22 sources, 25 claims adversarially verified, 0 refuted — see the memo) confirms the reframe on strong evidence:

  • Light is multidimensional, not good/bad. Hardness, direction, colour, intensity — an artistic choice. "Neither is inherently better than the other" (The Honcho); "there may not always be a 'best' quality of light" (Photography Life).
  • Landscape is highly light-dependent (low-angle golden/blue; midday/overcast flatten it) — while street is light-flexible: "you can shoot street in literally any light" (DPS); a working shooter reports "~95% of my photos are not during golden hour."
  • Harsh/flat is a creative mode for street, not a penalty — Fan Ho's chiaroscuro, Metzker's high-contrast B&W, overcast for even-light detail.
  • Precedent exists: Golden Hour One splits its score into a Sky Index and a "Light Index for portraits, landscape and architecture" — genre-differentiated, and pointedly not street.
The nuance we must respect — street is low, not zero

Color / light-forward street does time light. Alex Webb: "I work in color, where light is really important… I am always out at the latter half of the afternoon and in the evening." Fan Ho scouted light deliberately. So light should be allowed to nudge street a little — never to gate it.

03

The decision

Light quality becomes genre-dependent. Replace the single BASE_QUALITY ladder with two per-genre inputs:

  • Light-sensitivity 0..1 — how much the light phase is allowed to move a genre's score. High for landscape, low for street.
  • Phase-fit — how well each phase (golden / blue / flat / night) suits that genre's work. A map of creative fit, not a quality ranking.

Blended so a low-sensitivity genre stays near full score regardless of phase:

// windowType = the spot's light window (golden/blue/flat); REF_FIT = the reference
// fit for a fully light-sensitive genre. genre supplies the sensitivity.
phaseScore = 1 - sensitivity[genre] * (1 - REF_FIT[windowType])
light      = phaseScore * timing * weather

For street (sensitivity ≈ 0.15) phaseScore stays ~0.95–1.0 in any light — so light barely gates the go-decision, which leans on events, gear, and novelty instead. For landscape (≈ 0.9) phase-fit dominates — golden scores high, flat low, because there it genuinely is.

04

The model — proposed weights

The five researched genres are Strong; the rest are a reasoned extension (flagged). The numbers are a starting point to calibrate, not gospel — no source supplies weights.

What shipped (v1): a single shared REF_FIT (golden 1.0 · blue 0.85 · flat 0.55 — the old BASE_QUALITY) blended by per-genre LIGHT_SENSITIVITY, so a spot inherits its sensitivity from its primary genre. This captures the core fix (street not penalized, landscape still is) with only 8 new numbers. Deferred to calibration: a full per-genre phaseFit matrix that could invert the ordering (e.g. Nature/Details preferring overcast over golden) — v1 can only scale the golden>flat spread, not flip it.

06

Consequences

  • Shipped in lib/nudge.ts — LIGHT_SENSITIVITY (per genre) + a shared REF_FIT; BASE_QUALITY[windowType] swapped for the blended phaseScore(genre, windowType). 6 new tests (flat-light street not down-ranked; flat street > flat landscape; golden = reference top). 125 tests total, type-clean.
  • Resolves the incoherence — the scorer and the shoot brief finally agree that flat/overcast is fine for street.
  • The go-decision for street leans elsewhere — with light near-neutral, "is tonight worth it?" for a street shooter rests more on events, gear-fit, and variety. That's the correct product behaviour, and it makes the events layer (B4) more load-bearing.
  • Pairs with the Shoot-Brief ADR — that doc's phase→creative-mode mapping is the "what to shoot" layer; this is the "how much light gates it" layer. Same worldview, two jobs.
07

Open questions — calibration

  • The exact sensitivity numbers and the full phaseFit matrix — a starting point here, to tune against real shooting. No source supplies them.
  • What happens to windowType? Resolved — retained as the phase key: phaseScore(genre, windowType). windowType supplies the reference fit (which window), genre supplies how much that matters. It still also drives windowFor/timing (when the spot is live).
  • Multi-genre spots. A spot's genres[] can span Street + Architecture. Take the primary genre's sensitivity, the max, or a blend?
  • Interaction with weather. The cloudFactor tempering is itself phase-aware; confirm the two don't double-count once phase-fit lands.
  • The color-street exception — should a spot tagged as color/light-forward street carry a slightly higher sensitivity than gritty B&W street? (Webb vs. the snapshot tradition.)
