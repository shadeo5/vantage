UX Audit · Design Review

Vantage — a full UX review

A screen-by-screen read of the app, judged against its own mission — nudge people out to shoot, inspire don't nag, own the life not the light. The headline: this is a genuinely strong, cohesive product, not a prototype pretending to be one. The work now is closing the last gaps between "impressive demo" and "feels finished."

Reviewed Today · Detail · Plan · Bag Surface web preview (phone-scale) Findings 2 P0 · 10 P1 · 7 P2 Date Jul 13, 2026

First — what's genuinely working

Lead with the truth: most of this is good, and it should be protected, not "fixed."

Strengths to keep
  • The aesthetic is premium and coherent. Warm golden-hour palette, editorial serif headers, restrained gold accent — it reads like a real product with a point of view, not a template.
  • The voice is excellent and consistent. "Grab your 35mm and go," "Where the good frames hide," the honest gear lines — specific, warm, present tense. It matches VOICE.md and it makes the app feel like a friend, not a utility.
  • Gear-awareness is real and threaded everywhere. The pick, the "why," the fit labels all reflect your actual kit. That's the differentiator, and it lands.
  • The nudge-first hierarchy is clear. One confident pick on top, options beneath. The screen answers "where do I go tonight?" in one glance.
  • Lovely differentiated details. The light-quality ramp chart, the illustrated spot art, the "why this pick" breakdown — these are the moments people screenshot.

Today

The heart of the app — the nudge moment. Strong bones; a few things quietly undercut the "inspire" job.

P1 · #T1

The "Add your gear" banner persists after gear is added

Your bag already holds a Sony a7 IV + 35mm, yet the top banner still says "Add your gear — picks matched to the lens in your bag." It's the first thing you see, and it's telling you to do something you've done. It reads as broken.

→ Fix: only show it when gear is empty; once set, either drop it or flip it to a quiet, dismissible "Tuned to your a7 IV · edit." (This is the G3 re-prompt story — dismiss with a floor, re-ask on real events, not every launch.)
P1 · #T2

"TONIGHT'S PICK · MEDIUM" surfaces internal confidence as a flat label

On the one screen whose entire job is to excite you into going, the badge says "MEDIUM." To a user that reads as "eh, maybe." The confidence score is great engineering, but exposed raw it dampens the very feeling the pick should create.

→ Fix: reserve a visible flourish for HIGH nights only ("TONIGHT'S PICK ✦"), show nothing extra on medium, and let a genuinely weak night become the honest "quiet night" state instead of a labeled "MEDIUM." Never make the user grade their own evening a C.
P1 · #T3

"Activity — Live event signal, coming soon" shows a placeholder to the user

Inside "Why this pick?", the Activity line literally says "coming soon." Honest internally, but to a user it's an admission that a third of the reasoning isn't built. It makes the product feel unfinished at its most persuasive moment.

→ Fix: hide the Activity row until events exist, rather than showing an IOU. Two strong reasons (Light, Your kit) beat two strong + one "coming soon."
P2 · #T4

The avatar shows "M" — and goes nowhere

Top-right avatar reads "M" (a leftover mock initial; you're Desha), and tapping it does nothing — there's no profile/account. In a personalization-first app, the wrong initial is a small credibility ding.

→ Fix: derive the initial (or drop the avatar until there's a profile to open). If it stays, it should lead somewhere — even just to the Bag/settings.
P2 · #T5

The Today→Detail transition flashed black on web

Opening a spot showed a black frame mid-animation (the opacity + translateY entrance). May be a web-only artifact, but a black flash on every open would feel janky.

→ Fix: verify on the Pixel; if present, cross-fade from the current screen rather than fading up from black, and confirm the hero image is loaded before animating.

Spot Detail

The best-looking screen in the app. Two functional gaps.

P1 · #D1

The heart/save looks functional but doesn't persist

The save heart toggles visually but forgets on reload (persistence was deferred). A control that looks real but silently does nothing is worse than no control — it quietly teaches users the app doesn't remember them.

→ Fix: either wire it to local + cloud storage (you already have both patterns), or remove it until there's a Saved surface to hold the results.
P2 · #D2

The light chart is beautiful but under-explained

"When the light works" with a ramping bar chart + Golden/Blue/Flat legend is a standout — but a non-obsessive shooter may not read what the bars mean (quality over the day) or spot the "now" marker.

→ Fix: one plain-language line ("taller = better light; you're here →") turns a pretty chart into a understood one. Make the "now" bar unmistakable.

Plan

Conceptually right (browse/commit for the week), but it's the most "demo" screen — and it has a factual bug.

P0 · #P1

The week is hardcoded — and the day labels are wrong

It's Monday, but the first card says "TONIGHT · SAT." The whole week (Sat / Sun / Wed / Fri, fixed spots, fixed reasons) is static placeholder data that never changes. The moment a user notices the day is wrong, the app's credibility as a "live" tool takes a real hit — this is the same trust the nudge depends on.

→ Fix (near-term): at minimum, generate day labels from the real date so "tonight" is actually tonight. Longer-term: the week should come from the same brain that drives Today, ranked out over the next several days' light — not a hand-typed list.
P1 · #P2

Four full-width gold "I'm going" buttons flatten the hierarchy

Every card carries a big, full-bleed gold "I'm going" bar. Gold is your accent — the color that's supposed to mean "this, now." Repeated four times at full size, it stops signaling anything and the screen feels heavy and same-y.

→ Fix: demote the per-card action to a smaller, secondary control (outline or a compact pill), and reserve the big solid-gold CTA for the single primary nudge. Let the eye rest.
P2 · #P3

"fits your 35mm" repeats on every card

The fit label is a nice touch, but identical on all cards it becomes wallpaper. (Correct — your kit does fit them all — but visually redundant.)

→ Fix: show the fit label only when it differs from the obvious (e.g. "grab a tele" on a spot your kit doesn't cover), so it earns attention when it appears.

Bag

The clearest UX problem in the app lives here: an onboarding screen wearing a navigation tab's clothes.

P0 · #B1

The Bag tab is still an onboarding step

Tapping "Bag" in the nav — to just check or tweak your gear — drops you into "SETUP · 2 OF 3," with "Continue" and "Skip — I'll add later" at the bottom. But there's no 3-step setup flow (the app opens on Today), so "2 OF 3" points at steps that don't exist, and "Skip / Continue" imply a wizard you're not in. It's disorienting every time you visit the tab.

→ Fix: split the two jobs. Keep a first-run onboarding if you want one, but the Bag tab should be a calm gear-manager: title "Your bag," no step counter, and a single "Done" (or nothing — changes auto-save, which they already do). Kill "Skip — I'll add later" on a destination you chose to open.
P1 · #B2

No real first-run path to setting gear

New user lands on Today (good). To set gear they tap the "Add your gear" banner → Bag → which then shows the confusing wizard chrome above. So the one onboarding action funnels into the one broken-feeling screen.

→ Fix: decide the model. Simplest: no wizard — Today works out of the box with sensible defaults, and Bag is always just gear management. The banner (when gear is unset) becomes a gentle "personalize your picks →," not a mandatory step.
P2 · #B3

"YOUR KIT SHOOTS" is quietly one of the best moments — surface it more

Changing the camera/lenses live-updates the genre chips (Street · Architecture). It's a delightful "the app gets my gear" beat that's currently buried below the fold. This is a strength hiding as an afterthought.

→ Fix: give it a touch more prominence (it's the payoff of the whole screen), and consider echoing it on Today ("your kit's built for street tonight").

Native feel & interaction

Found by Desha using it on the Pixel — the clearest proof that on-device beats screenshots. These are what separate "a website in an app shell" from "a native app."

P1 · #N1

No swipe navigation between tabs

You can only change tabs by tapping the bottom bar. On a phone, swiping left/right between Today · Plan · Bag is a reflex — its absence is one of the biggest "this feels like a website" tells.

→ Fix: make the three tabs a horizontal pager (swipe + the bottom bar both drive it, kept in sync). Building this now.
P1 · #N2

Bottom-tab icons get lost

The nav icons are thin, low-contrast line-art on a dark ground — they recede, and the active/inactive difference is subtle, so the bar reads as faint text more than a tab bar.

→ Fix: heavier strokes + brighter inactive color, a clearer active state (fill/gold + a small indicator), and slightly larger icons. Building this now.
P1 · #N3

Buttons feel web, not mobile

Taps have no press feedback — no dim, no scale, no Android ripple. On a phone, a button that doesn't visibly react to your finger feels dead/web-like. This is pervasive (every button), so fixing it lifts the whole app at once.

→ Fix: add a pressed state (opacity/scale) + Android ripple to the primary controls and chips. Building this now.
P2 · #N4

No overscroll bounce at the top/bottom

Scrolling to the end just stops flat. The rubber-band bounce (iOS) / stretch (Android 12+) is a small thing your hand expects; without it, scrolling feels rigid.

→ Fix: enable bounce/stretch overscroll on the scroll views. Building this now.

Cross-cutting themes

Patterns that span screens.

  • Demo seams vs. real data. The recurring trust-leak is placeholder data surfacing as if it's live: the Plan week, the "M" avatar, "coming soon." Each is small; together they whisper "not finished." Prioritize replacing visible placeholders over invisible ones.
  • Controls that don't remember. Save (heart) and — verify — any other visual-only toggles. A nudge app's whole promise is "it knows you"; a control that forgets undermines that more than its size suggests.
  • Guard the "inspire, don't nag" line. "MEDIUM," the persistent gear banner, "coming soon" — individually minor, but they're all small downward pressures on the exact feeling the app sells. Polishing these is high-leverage for the mission.
  • Accent discipline. Gold is doing a lot of jobs (CTAs, highlights, active nav, chart bars). It's mostly well-handled, but Plan's four gold buttons show what happens when the accent over-spends. Keep solid gold for "the one thing to do now."
  • The come-back loop is thin. Nudge → shoot → return → discovery is the strategy, but on return there's little new to discover (Plan is static, no fresh content). The return moment (check-in + journal) works; what it returns you to needs freshness to close the loop.

Needs on-device evaluation

You have it on the Pixel now — these can only be judged in the hand, and they're where "experience" really lives.

  • The notification itself. How the nudge actually reads on the lock screen — title/body truncation, the app icon, sound, whether it earns the tap. This is the product's front door; screenshot it and scrutinize it.
  • Touch targets & thumb reach. The lens chips, the small "why" chevron, the back/heart circles — are they comfortably tappable one-handed? Is the primary action in the thumb zone?
  • Safe areas. The sticky "I'm going" vs. the Pixel's gesture bar; the header vs. the status bar / notch. Verify nothing is clipped or fighting the system UI.
  • Motion & haptics. Do "I'm going," toggles, and tab switches have haptic feedback? Do the animations feel native or webby? (See the black-flash note.)
  • Contrast in real light. The muted-grey-on-dark labels look fine on a monitor; check them outdoors, at dusk, one-handed — the actual context of use.
  • Permission timing. The notification-permission prompt fires on launch; consider asking it in-context ("want a nudge when tonight's good?") right after the first great pick, when the value is obvious.

The bottom line

Vantage already feels like something — the hard part (a real voice, a coherent look, a working brain that reaches your phone) is done and done well. What's left is unglamorous but high-leverage: remove the visible demo seams, make every control keep its promise, and protect the "inspire" feeling from small downward tugs. None of the P0/P1 items are big builds — they're the difference between "wow, a prototype" and "wait, this is a real app I'd use." Start with the Bag tab and the Plan dates; they're the two that actively contradict reality.
